# Which one worked? Legendeering rounds (v9)

Built 2026-09-10 on the v8 engine after Evan's correction that Talex does not make commercials. Seven two-sided rounds, each a Legendeering approach against the old way, six of them from Tom's Value First newsletter. Prototype, draft for Tom.

## What it is

The landing page is deliberately short: the gate quote from Tom's "What's 100 Years Old is New Again" and the seven rounds as a list, each a link (`#r1` to `#r7`) that opens that round's game. A round is four screens, the same as the live case study: pick one of two cards, see the podium (gold and silver) with your pick beside the real one, answer one "why did it work?" question, read the source and the takeaway. Two points a round, fourteen in all, with the points pill, streak, tier and confetti carried over from v8.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | The page and the engine. Hash routes, one render path per screen. Embeds play muted. |
| `content.js` | Every word. `SIDES` (fourteen sides, two per round), `ROUNDS`, `COPY`. Regenerated by the build. |
| `build-content.js` | Copies the two library sides (Rico's Tacos, Second Act) byte for byte from `format-blocks.json`, writes the authored sides and rounds, and stamps a content hash onto the script URL in `index.html`. Run it after any copy change. |

## The rounds

1. Own the show, or rent thirty seconds in someone else's. Rico's Tacos (library block) against a rented slot. Tom, Welcome to The Taco Drama.
2. A series about them, or an ad about you. Specsavers' Best Worst Team (official channel, episode confirmed via oEmbed) against the slogan on TV. The Drum.
3. Make your own shows, or sponsor other people's. Mailchimp's Second Act (library block) against sponsoring. Digiday.
4. Every week for years, or two years of silence. YouTube creators against a Netflix hit. Tom, Bigger Isn't Always Better.
5. Give it away, or beg three thousand drivers to buy tires. Michelin's 1900 guide against tire ads. Tom, Where the Rubber Meets The Restaurant.
6. Answer the real question, or stuff the keyword. HubSpot against keyword stuffing. Tom, Keywords Don't Fix Leaky Pipes.
7. Show your humanity, or let the machine make it. Polaroid against machine-made content. Tom, Breaking: Humans Still Prefer Humans.

Sides that are ways of working rather than films (a rented slot, keyword stuffing) show a designed poster in the brand colours with the line "No film to show". Nothing is faked.

## Rules kept

Quotes verbatim from the source named on each round. The "why" answers are prepared by Talex and labelled not yet approved by Tom. No commercial appears as a Talex example; a commercial appears only as the thing the show beat. Same palette, fonts and logo as the strategy template. Footer address info@talexmedia.com.
